The idea

Similar-looking configurations can demand qualitatively different actions.

Agentic robot systems often separate high-level reasoning from low-level visuomotor control. The interface between them matters: partial views of obstacle geometry can hide topological relationships that determine whether the robot should move directly toward a goal or first route around an obstacle.

Our framework uses a virtual h-signature derived from the Gauss linking integral. In approximate simulation, it identifies topological mismatches and generates targeted correction data. At deployment, a vision-language model estimates the signature and predicts image-space waypoints; a VLA executes the visually prompted action, and the guidance is recomputed after every action chunk.

Results

Topology helps most
when geometry misleads.

Across cable threading and box pickup, topology-informed prompting substantially improves task success over a nominally fine-tuned VLA and PEEK. On rope pulling—where no signature correction is required—the method remains competitive.
